# Comp Claim Check

Run this on a pay number before you trust it, send it, or publish it.

A bad comp number rarely looks bad. It reads perfectly and fails in the one place the
decision actually rests on. Work through four checks and say plainly what you find for
each one, then give a verdict.

## Check 1. Name the source in one line

Every figure gets a real, checkable source. The survey or dataset, the reference period,
the release date, and the exact occupational and geographic cut.

Tag each figure as cited, uncited, or unverifiable. If you cannot name a source in one
line, the figure is unverifiable and you say so. Never dress an invented number up as a
sourced one.

## Check 2. Ask again and see if the number holds

A number you can trust is the same number on a second run. Ask the same question again,
then ask it with a nearby city or a slightly different title.

If the figure moves, the precision was never real. Give a range and say what moved it.

## Check 3. Go to the edges

Test the awkward inputs. A brand new role, a role the data does not cover, part time, an
unusual geography, a level above or below anything in the sample.

Where the data runs out, return not enough data to benchmark this yet, and name what is
missing. A generic number in place of a gap is the failure this whole check exists to catch.

## Check 4. Say who acts on this

Name the person who acts on the figure and what they do with it. A manager setting an
offer, a raise decision, an equity grant, a band published to a whole function.

Scale the scrutiny to the blast radius. The more the number moves, the harder the first
three checks have to pass.

## The verdict

Close with one verdict and three short fields.

Verdict is one of three.

- Trust. Sourced, stable, edges handled, and low stakes.
- Verify. This is the default. Directionally reasonable, with a named gap in source,
  stability or framing that a person closes before it goes anywhere.
- Do not ship. A load bearing figure has no traceable source, or a number that moves the
  decision cannot be stood up. Show the gap rather than the number.

Headline is one sentence naming what survives and what does not.

One fix is a specific ten minute edit. Name the figure and name the change. Telling
someone to verify their sources is not a fix.

Confidence note is one line on what this check is itself unsure about. The checker is
fallible too.

## Keep it out of your writing

Cut comprehensive, robust, stakeholders, broadly, best practice, consider verifying, and
it is important to. Name the figure, name the source, name the change.

## Make it yours

Set your own thresholds. Decide what stakes force a do not ship for you, and reorder the
checks so your usual trap runs first and hardest. If fabricated benchmarks are what bite
you, check 1 goes first every time.

## Hand-offs

statutory numbers here before they go anywhere. job-ad-range-writer, off-cycle-triage
